AI is designing OpenAI's next model in a sign of 'super intelligence': SoftBank's Masayoshi Son to CNBC

Alphabet is seeking fresh capital as stock's 4-week losing streak tests investor appetite

Alphabet is seeking USD85bn in fresh capital to fund its artificial intelligence build-out, as its stock faces a 4-week losing streak, the longest in over a year. The company's market capitalization briefly surpassed Nvidia's a month ago, but has since declined, with shares up 120% over the past year despite the recent pullback. Alphabet has already secured over USD55bn in fresh debt since November and upped its capital expenditures guidance to as high as USD190bn for the year. The equity raise includes a USD10bn investment from Berkshire Hathaway, and comes as Melius Research estimates Google's free cash flow will turn negative for the next few years due to AI capex. Shares have fallen as investors question the company's AI progress, particularly in coding models, and express concerns over its USD190bn capex plans.

Alphabet's decision to raise capital may be driven by the upcoming initial public offerings of SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI, which are expected to raise significant amounts of capital, potentially diverting investor attention and funds away from Alphabet. The company's AI investments are likely to continue, with a focus on advancing its models and competing with offerings from Anthropic and OpenAI, and the fresh capital will help preserve financial flexibility while accelerating spending. With a valuation of over USD1.3tn, Alphabet's capital raise is likely to have significant implications for the tech industry, particularly in the AI sector, where companies are spending heavily on infrastructure and research. Investors are focused on the company's ability to execute on its AI strategy and generate returns on its significant investments, with the stock's valuation multiples likely to be impacted by the outcome.
