Google loses fight over record $4.7 billion EU antitrust fine

Google lost its appeal against a record eu antitrust fine of eur4.1bn (usd4.7bn). The european court of justice upheld the fine, which was imposed in 2018 for alleged anti-competitive practices related to android's mobile dominance. Google's shares fell 1% in premarket trading. The company has no further right to appeal. Google has argued that android provides choice for users and supports developers and businesses across europe.

The european commission has been pursuing google for over a decade, with google also facing a eur2.95bn fine last year for anti-competitive practices in its advertising technology business. The regulator is now focusing on big technology firms under the digital markets act and digital services act, with companies like apple and meta also under scrutiny. The decision marks the end of the commission's "first stage" battle with big tech, with a new focus on legislative tools to regulate big tech. Google has adapted its agreements to comply with the initial decision and remains focused on innovation and openness for users, partners, and developers.
