AMD buys chip startup that hardwires AI models into its silicon

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) is acquiring Taalas, a startup that makes chips for artificial intelligence (AI) inference, for an undisclosed price. Taalas has raised USD219m in venture funding since its founding in 2023. The deal comes after Nvidia spent USD20bn on Groq, a designer of high-performance AI chips. Taalas' technology promises a less-expensive chip that can produce output for specific models thousands of times faster than a traditional graphics processing unit (GPU). AMD will integrate the Taalas chips and technology into its road map, including in systems with its central processors and Instinct GPUs.

The acquisition reflects the rising importance for leading GPU makers to offer integrated systems with several different components and chips. AMD recently started shipping Helios, its first rack-scale rival to Nvidia's integrated server racks, to customers including Meta and Microsoft. The deal also highlights the growing demand for alternative chips like those from Taalas and Groq, particularly for "low-latency" applications. AMD's market cap is significantly lower than Nvidia's, which has reached over USD5tn, but the acquisition is a strategic move to expand its offerings in the AI chip market. The integration of Taalas' technology is expected to enhance AMD's competitive positioning in the market.
